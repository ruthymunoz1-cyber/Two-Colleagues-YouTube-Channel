## Phrase Bank (the 8 phrases this episode teaches — whiteboard shows them ONLY in the recap)

1. "I'm so sorry about that."
2. "You're right — that's not what you ordered."
3. "Let me fix that for you right away."
4. "Thank you for your patience."
5. "I completely understand."
6. "Would you like to speak with my manager?"
7. "This one is on us." *(only with manager approval)*
8. "Is there anything else I can make right for you?"

## COLD OPEN — HOOK (0:00–0:25)

**[SCENE: Dinner service. Maria sets a plate down at a table. VOICE OF CUSTOMER = James's voice, sharp and loud.]**

**CUSTOMER (angry, loud):** This is WRONG. I said NO cheese. Do you people even listen? I've been waiting thirty minutes for the wrong food!

**[FREEZE FRAME on Maria mid-flinch.]**

**MARIA (VO):** When someone yells at you in your second language, your English doesn't just get worse — it disappears.

**MARIA (VO):** Tonight, my manager taught me the phrases that calm almost anyone down. By the end of this video, you'll handle this exact customer — calmly. Watch.

---

## PART 1 — THE FRAME (0:25–1:10)

**[SCENE: Back of house, quiet corner near the office. James hands Maria a glass of water. She's shaken.]**

**JAMES:** Hey. You okay? Table 12 was rough.

**MARIA:** I froze, James. Everything I wanted to say — gone. And in English it's worse. It feels… personal.

**JAMES:** Listen to me, because this is the most important thing I'll say tonight: an angry customer is not angry at YOU. They're angry at the situation. Your job isn't to win — it's to lower the temperature.

**MARIA:** Lower the temperature?

**JAMES (counting on his fingers):** Three moves. One — don't match their energy. Two — agree before you explain. Three — fix it, or hand it up to me. That's the whole ladder. Want to practice? I'll be your worst customer.

**MARIA (small laugh):** You're scary when you do voices.

---

## PART 2 — SKILL 1: DON'T MATCH THEIR ENERGY (1:10–2:40)

**[SCENE: Empty dining room, after close. James sits at a table as the customer. Maria approaches.]**

**JAMES (as himself, aside):** Rule one is physical, not English: when they get loud, you get SLOWER and QUIETER. It feels wrong. Do it anyway — it's almost impossible to keep yelling at a calm person.

**JAMES (as customer, loud):** This soup is COLD. Cold! Is the kitchen even on?

**MARIA (fast, panicked):** Oh no, sorry, sorry, I—

**JAMES (as himself):** Stop. You sped up. Speed reads as panic, and panic tells them nobody's in control. Again — slow, low, and start with the phrase: *"I'm so sorry about that."*

*(KEY PHRASE 1 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as customer, loud):** This soup is COLD!

**MARIA (slow, steady):** I'm so sorry about that.

**JAMES (as himself):** Feel the difference? Four words, said slowly, and YOU are the calmest person at the table. One warning: apologize ONCE. If you keep repeating "sorry, sorry, sorry," it stops meaning anything.

---

## PART 3 — SKILL 2: AGREE BEFORE YOU EXPLAIN (2:40–4:20)

**JAMES (as himself):** Now the counter-move nobody teaches. When they say what's wrong — even rudely — your first sentence AGREES with them. The phrase: *"You're right — that's not what you ordered."*

*(KEY PHRASE 2 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as customer):** I ordered this with NO cheese. It's covered in cheese!

**MARIA:** You're right — that's not what you ordered.

**JAMES (as customer, deflating slightly):** …Right. Exactly.

**JAMES (as himself):** Did you hear the fight leave his voice? You can't argue with someone who agrees with you. What you never do is explain first — "the kitchen was busy," "the printer cut the ticket" — the customer hears excuses, and excuses are gasoline.

**JAMES (as himself):** Then, immediately, the fix. The phrase: *"Let me fix that for you right away."*

*(KEY PHRASE 3 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** You're right — that's not what you ordered. Let me fix that for you right away.

**JAMES (as himself):** Agree, then act. Two sentences, no essay. And when you bring the new plate, close the wait with: *"Thank you for your patience."* Not "sorry for the wait" — you already apologized once. Thank them instead; it tells them they behaved well, and people live up to that.

*(KEY PHRASE 4 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Thank you for your patience.

---

## PART 4 — SKILL 3: FIX IT OR HAND IT UP (4:20–5:50)

**JAMES (as himself):** Last skill. Some customers stay angry. That's not failure — that's your cue for the ladder. First rung: empathy at full strength: *"I completely understand."*

*(KEY PHRASE 5 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as customer, still hot):** This ruined our whole evening. My wife's food is finished and mine hasn't even started!

**MARIA:** I completely understand.

**JAMES (as himself):** Second rung — offer me. That's not defeat, Maria. Bringing the manager is a professional MOVE, not a surrender. The phrase: *"Would you like to speak with my manager?"*

*(KEY PHRASE 6 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Would you like to speak with my manager?

**JAMES (as himself):** And rung three — sometimes I'll authorize this: *"This one is on us."* That means free — the restaurant pays. Hear me clearly: you NEVER say it without my okay. But when I give it, you deliver it like a gift, not like a fine we're paying.

*(KEY PHRASE 7 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** This one is on us.

**JAMES (as himself):** Then the closer — the phrase that turns a bad night into a returning customer: *"Is there anything else I can make right for you?"*

*(KEY PHRASE 8 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Is there anything else I can make right for you?

**JAMES (as himself, warm):** That question tells them someone finally cares. Nine times out of ten, they say "no… no, we're okay" — and they mean it.

---

## PART 5 — THE PAYOFF (5:50–6:30)

**[SCENE: Return to the cold-open moment. Same table, same energy.]**

**MARIA (VO):** Remember table 12?

**JAMES (as customer, loud — same line as cold open):** This is WRONG. I said NO cheese. Do you people even listen?

**MARIA (slow, low, steady):** I'm so sorry about that. You're right — that's not what you ordered. Let me fix that for you right away.

**JAMES (as customer, cooling):** …Okay. Yeah. Thank you.

**[CUT: Maria returns with the corrected plate.]**

**MARIA:** Here you are — no cheese. Thank you for your patience. Is there anything else I can make right for you?

**JAMES (as customer, almost embarrassed):** No, this is… this is great, actually. Sorry I got loud.

**MARIA (to camera, quiet smile):** They apologized to ME.
