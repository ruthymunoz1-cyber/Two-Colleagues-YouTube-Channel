## COLD OPEN — HOOK (0:00–0:30)

**[SCENE: Shift change. Maria, exhausted, apron half off, bag on shoulder. James walks in tying his apron — he's covering the close tonight. Played for comedy.]**

**JAMES:** Okay — what do I need to know?

**MARIA (already halfway to the door, vague hand-wave):** Um… table 9 is… something? The bar guy is… you'll see. Oh and there's a thing with the kitchen. Bye!

**[FREEZE FRAME on James's face: horror.]**

**JAMES (VO, deadpan):** She just handed me a haunted house and left.

**MARIA (VO):** THAT was my handover last month. Nobody teaches this: at the end of your shift, you have sixty seconds to summarize hours of chaos — in English. Tonight, we fix it.

## PART 1 — THE FRAME (0:30–1:20)

**[SCENE: Rewind chime — earlier that evening. Quiet moment at the service station. James, mentoring.]**

**JAMES:** Maria, you've learned to take orders, calm angry customers, speak kitchen, even call in sick like a pro. Tonight's the last skill — and it's the one that gets people promoted.

**MARIA:** The handover?

**JAMES:** The handover. Here's why it matters: it's the only moment where you're not answering questions — you're the one giving the briefing. Summarizing. Prioritizing. In English. Do it well and you sound like a leader. Do it like last month and—

**MARIA:** —the haunted house. I know. I was there.

**JAMES (grinning):** Three rules. One: urgent first. Two: one sentence per table. Three: close the loop before you leave. That's the whole system.

---

## PART 2 — SKILL 1: URGENT FIRST — THE HANDOVER ORDER (1:20–3:00)

**JAMES:** A handover isn't a story — it's a priority list, out loud. Always the same order: what's URGENT, what's IN PROGRESS, what's just GOOD TO KNOW.

**MARIA:** Not in the order things happened?

**JAMES:** Never. Nobody needs your shift as a movie. They need it as a list. And you open every handover the same way — it tells the other person to start listening NOW:

*(KEY PHRASE 1 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Here's what you need to know.

**JAMES:** Then straight to urgent. What's the most on-fire thing right now?

**MARIA (thinking):** Dessert for 9 has been slow…

*(KEY PHRASE 2 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Table 9 is still waiting on their dessert.

**JAMES:** Urgent, specific, one sentence. Then in-progress and good-to-know, like: which tables are finished?

*(KEY PHRASE 3 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** The couple at table 3 already paid.

**JAMES:** See what that sentence does? It just saved me from asking table 3 for money they already spent. One sentence — one embarrassment prevented.

---

## PART 3 — SKILL 2: ONE SENTENCE PER TABLE (3:00–4:40)

**JAMES:** Rule two, the summarizing muscle: every table gets ONE sentence — its status plus what happens next. Try table 6. And don't tell me the story — I know there was a whole thing.

**MARIA (starting to ramble, demo):** Okay so table 6, the woman, she asked about the sauce, and I wasn't sure, so I went to ask, and Chef said—

**JAMES (hand up, kind):** Story.

**MARIA (beat — then one clean sentence):**

*(KEY PHRASE 6 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** I already told the kitchen about the allergy at table 6.

**JAMES:** THAT'S the sentence. Status AND "you don't need to do anything." Half of a great handover is telling me what I *don't* have to worry about. Now — restaurant-wide news. Anything the whole floor needs?

**MARIA (small smile — she knows this language now):**

*(KEY PHRASE 4 — spoken repetition, callback to Video 3)*

**MARIA:** We're 86 salmon tonight.

**JAMES (proud):** Kitchen English, week two. Excellent. And the human intel — the stuff no computer knows?

*(KEY PHRASE 5 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** The guy at the bar is a regular — he always orders the same thing. Old Fashioned, no cherry. Don't offer him a menu, he thinks it's funny to say no.

**JAMES (laughs):** That sentence is worth more than the till. Regulars remember the server who already knew.

---

## PART 4 — SKILL 3: CLOSE THE LOOP (4:40–5:40)

**JAMES:** Last rule. The difference between leaving and abandoning is one question. Before you walk out, you ask:

*(KEY PHRASE 7 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Anything you need from me before I go?

**JAMES:** That question is your exit visa. Maybe I ask one thing — where the dessert menus went, whatever — thirty seconds. But now leaving is a DECISION we both made, not a disappearance. And when you're the one RECEIVING a handover, you release the other person the same way:

*(KEY PHRASE 8 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES:** You're all set. Have a good shift!

**MARIA:** So the handover has a beginning — "here's what you need to know" — and an ending — "anything you need from me?" It's… a little ceremony.

**JAMES:** A sixty-second ceremony that makes two strangers into two colleagues. *(beat)* Someone should name a channel that.

## PART 5 — THE PAYOFF: THE 60-SECOND MODEL HANDOVER (5:40–6:40)

**[SCENE: End of the night — mirror of the cold open. Maria, bag on shoulder, apron off. James tying his. Same setup, new Maria. One continuous take; a subtle clock in frame ticks ~60 seconds.]**

**JAMES:** Okay — what do I need to know?

**MARIA (calm, ordered):** Here's what you need to know. Urgent: table 9 is still waiting on their dessert — kitchen says two minutes, it's on me to have told you. In progress: table 12 just ordered mains, and the couple at table 3 already paid, they're just finishing their wine. Good to know: we're 86 salmon tonight. I already told the kitchen about the allergy at table 6 — nothing left to do there. And the guy at the bar is a regular — he always orders the same thing. Old Fashioned, no cherry.

**JAMES (nodding slowly):** …That might be the cleanest handover I've ever gotten.

**MARIA:** Anything you need from me before I go?

**JAMES:** Nothing. You're all set — have a good night, Maria.

**MARIA (to camera, walking out, the door chime rings):** Sixty seconds. Every table safe. THAT'S how you leave work.
