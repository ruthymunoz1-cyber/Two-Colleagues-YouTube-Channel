### Card 1 — Blamed at the table for the kitchen's mistake

1. **The story:** Servers carry the blame for kitchen delays and wrong orders, and workers with limited English have no polite script for absorbing/redirecting that blame without either taking the fall or sounding defensive. The "it wasn't my mistake" walk to the kitchen is one of the most viral recurring formats in worker shortform right now — in English AND Spanish.
2. **Evidence:**
   - IG @mexizol_rhodeisland, "That walk when it wasn't your mistake" / "When it's the kitchen fault and not yours..." — **426.3K views, 57.9x** creator median (reel:Datn6R6xUWE)
   - TikTok @insidezanesbrain, "WHERE IS MY BOLOGNESE? IT'S BEEN 35 MINUTES" restaurant-frustration skit — **395.2K views, 163.7x** median (https://www.tiktok.com/@insidezanesbrain/video/7657710242955906335)
   - IG @lafincarestaurantezacatecas (ES), "Cómo quieren los de cocina que reaccionen los meseros" (kitchen vs. servers) — **29.5K views, 16.9x** median on a 135-follower account (reel:DZut8ymOAEe)
   - Context: staffing-shortage coverage shows servers running larger sections with longer ticket times, so blame-for-delays is structural, not anecdotal (Paytronix / Toast 2026 labor-shortage reports).
3. **Demand score: 9/10** — cross-platform ×3 (IG-EN + TikTok-EN + IG-ES, three independent creators, same complaint); engagement high on tiny accounts (format outperforms follower count = topic-driven demand); keyword base solid ("english for restaurant workers" 3.3K/mo vs. competition 39); gap check: zero worker-side teaching content on this exact moment.
4. **Episode angle:** A table blames Maria loudly for a 35-minute wait that's the kitchen's backlog. James coaches her on owning the guest experience without taking personal blame — and on how to talk to the kitchen afterward without starting a war. Emotional beat: the injustice of wearing someone else's mistake, resolved with professional dignity. Candidate phrases: "I'm so sorry for the wait — let me check on that right now." / "You're right, that took too long. Let me make it right." / "Hey chef, table 12 is asking — what's the ETA on that bolognese?" / "It's not about whose fault it is — let's fix it together."
5. **Track + urgency:** Restaurant. EVERGREEN.
6. **Verdict: GO** — distinct from launch ep 2 (angry-customer de-escalation): this is specifically the blame-transfer moment and the FOH↔BOH conversation after it. Natural sibling to backlog #7.

### Card 2 — Kitchen call-outs are a secret language ("Corner!", "Behind, hot!")

1. **The story:** Kitchen safety call-outs and service jargon are their own language that no textbook teaches, and shortform audiences (worker and civilian) are obsessed with decoding them. For a new immigrant worker, not knowing "behind, hot" isn't embarrassing — it's dangerous.
2. **Evidence:**
   - IG @biancapaine, "behind hot but it's just you" — **334.8K views, 40.7x** median (reel:DarPovroPXR); references an earlier viral "Corner Hot" moment (trend has legs)
   - IG @theboroughdowningtown, coworkers act out literal meanings of "Corner!" and kitchen jargon — **368.5K views, 14.9x** median (reel:DaVjpCFJgGH)
   - IG @red.flame.workers, "Living between FOH & BOH" — **690.8K views, 239.5x** median on a 119-follower account; audience tagged as Hispanic/Latin American restaurant workers in the US (reel:DaJEI1qP_kR)
3. **Demand score: 8/10** — cross-platform repetition ×3 (three IG creators, one explicitly Latino-worker audience); very high engagement multiples; gap check: comedic decode content exists, teaching content for ESL workers does not; keyword base moderate.
4. **Episode angle:** Direct validation for launch ep 3 (Kitchen English) — no new episode needed, but the recap segment should drill the call-outs specifically ("Behind!", "Corner!", "Hot!", "Heard!", "86 the salmon", "All day"). Emotional beat: from flinching at shouts to shouting back with confidence. Candidate phrases: "Behind you, hot pan!" / "Heard, chef!" / "Corner!" / "How many salmon all day?"
5. **Track + urgency:** Restaurant. EVERGREEN.
6. **Verdict: GO — validates launch ep 3 (Kitchen English).** Fresh demand evidence attached; suggest the call-out drill becomes the whiteboard recap.

### Card 4 — The cold "Ok": getting recognition from your manager

1. **The story:** Workers post about bosses who reply "Ok" to a covered shift or a finished task when the worker hoped for a "thank you" — invisible-labor resentment is a top-performing emotional theme in Spanish worker shortform. Workers with limited English can't advocate for their own visibility: asking for feedback, flagging what they did, or receiving praise gracefully.
2. **Evidence:**
   - IG @nestor_r_vallejos_ (ES), "Cuando mi jefe me responde con un frío 'Ok'..." — **787.9K views, 54.6x** median on a 2.1K-follower account (reel:DaiInXQuzsE)
   - IG @bella_suiza (ES), "Yo trabajando con los audios que envía el jefe" (demanding boss voice-notes) — **71.7K views, 15.2x** median (reel:DaDw-VxK43k)
   - IG @tavernaopaorl (EN), manager/server banter about shift unpredictability — **69.6K views, 4.5x** median (reel:DZ-LIpIKoMB)
3. **Demand score: 7/10** — cross-platform ×3 (two ES + one EN, all worker-boss dynamics); strong engagement on the lead source; no direct keyword data (emotion, not a search query) — this is a discovery-feed topic, not a search topic; gap check: nothing teaches "how to talk to your manager about your work" for ESL workers.
4. **Episode angle:** Maria covers a Saturday shift, gets a flat "ok" text, and stews. James (as the manager who didn't realize) models both sides: how a worker can make their effort visible without bragging, and how to ask for feedback. Emotional beat: wanting to be SEEN, not just used. Candidate phrases: "Just so you know, I stayed late to close on Saturday." / "Could I get some feedback on how I'm doing?" / "It means a lot when you notice." / "Happy to help — next time I might need the favor returned."

### Card 5 — Working scared: immigration enforcement anxiety at work

1. **The story:** ICE workplace enforcement is at 2018 levels; Spanish-language "operativo" warning videos are pulling millions of views, and hospitality workers — including citizens and legal residents — report going "work to home, home to work," missing shifts out of fear. The workplace-communication edge of this: telling a manager you can't come in, asking about your rights at work, and managers/workers talking about safety.
2. **Evidence:**
   - TikTok @express.not (ES), ICE operations warning — **2.2M views, 205.6x** median (https://www.tiktok.com/@express.not/video/7659793833605074189)
   - TikTok @emmanuelcanooficial (ES), enforcement ops in public places — **1.4M views, 74.9x** median, 506.6K-follower news creator (https://www.tiktok.com/@emmanuelcanooficial/video/7660732308097486094)
   - News: SevenFifty Daily "hospitality workers on the front lines of ICE raids" (workers missing a week of work out of fear); Poached (May 2026) "restaurants are still dealing with the immigration crackdown"; backofhouse.io on business impact; American Immigration Council fact sheet. 40+ worksite actions, 1,100+ arrests reported in seven months.
3. **Demand score: 9/10** — highest raw engagement of the sweep, cross-platform ×3 (TikTok ES ×2 + sustained news cycle), trajectory rising. But demand is for NEWS/safety info, not English lessons — the overlap with our beat is real but narrow.
4. **Episode angle (if greenlit):** narrow and dignified — Maria hears about enforcement activity nearby and is anxious at work; the episode teaches calm workplace-communication phrases, not legal advice and not politics: "I don't feel safe coming in today." / "Who can I talk to if something happens during my shift?" / "Can we go over the plan if inspectors come in?" / "I'd like to keep this conversation private." The emotional beat is safety and being taken seriously.
5. **Track + urgency:** Restaurant (crosses all tracks). TIMELY — jumps the queue or dies.
6. **Verdict: WATCH — Ruthy's call required.** Demand data says 9; brand risk says pause. Dignity rule is satisfiable (episode is about what workers face), but the topic is politically charged and could define the channel before the channel has defined itself. Re-check next sweep; if the anxiety is still structural (it will be), decide deliberately, not opportunistically.

### Card 6 — Tips are down, tip rules changed: talking about money at work

1. **The story:** Two money stories collided in 2026: tipping fatigue is measurably shrinking tips (20%+ tippers down from 45% to 41%; restaurants the #1 place consumers say they're cutting), while the "no tax on tips" deduction (final rules confirmed 2026, up to $25K, 70+ occupations) has workers confused about paychecks, pooling, and what's actually tax-free. Workers need English to ask money questions without embarrassment.
2. **Evidence:**
   - RSM US: final no-tax-on-tips regs confirm qualifying occupations (https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2026/no-tax-tips-final-rules-confirm-qualifying-occupations-tip-definition.html)
   - TaxAct / Fidelity / splitty explainers all published for 2026 filing — an explainer arms race = search demand (https://blog.taxact.com/no-tax-on-tips-explained/)
   - Tipping fatigue data: KTLA "tipping culture reaches breaking point" survey; 8NewsNow "are Americans tipping less" (35% cutting at restaurants); TipCalc 2026 data roundup
   - State wrinkle: California and others don't conform — state tax still owed (ietaxattorney.com) — confusion is guaranteed
3. **Demand score: 7/10** — repetition is news-cycle-wide rather than worker-shortform (weaker on the ×3 axis for OUR audience); trajectory strong through tax season; gap check: every explainer is aimed at English-fluent taxpayers, none at ESL tipped workers; no direct keyword data pulled this sweep.
4. **Episode angle:** Validates backlog #6 (Talking About Tips & Splitting the Bill) and gives it a timely spine. Maria's tip-out is short and her paycheck looks different; she has to ask. Candidate phrases: "How does tip pooling work here?" / "Can you walk me through my paycheck?" / "Do I report cash tips separately?" / "I think there's a mistake in my tip-out."
5. **Track + urgency:** Restaurant. TIMELY (tax-season relevance recurs, but the "new rules" hook decays).
6. **Verdict: GO — validates backlog #6**; recommend it keeps a timely intro beat while the rules are news.

### Card 8 — "Me sentía tonta": the fear of speaking is the product

1. **The story:** The single pain point language-sellers pay Meta to poke is not vocabulary — it's shame: "every time I tried to speak English I felt dumb," "speak English without fear," targeted explicitly at #latinosenusa. The recurring worker moment is freezing mid-sentence and having no recovery script.
2. **Evidence:**
   - Winning Hunter Meta ads: **186 active ads** match "aprende ingles" (ES, US-targeted). Dominant advertiser SabioCasa runs copy like "Cada vez que intentaba hablar inglés me sentía tonta… hasta este libro", "En 3 semanas ya estás hablando inglés sin miedo", tags #InglésParaLatinos #mexicanaenusa #latinosenusa — and one ad specifically pitching restaurant phrases ("...es muy importante que aprendes estas frases! #restaurante")
   - vidIQ: "ingles para meseros" ~3,853/mo (comp 35.3), "ingles para restaurante" ~5,253/mo (comp 37) — people search for this in Spanish at healthy volume with low-mid competition
   - TikTok @nicolasacevedo.3c (ES), "¿Usted se siente atrasado en este país? 🇺🇸" immigrant encouragement — **293.7K views, 19.1x** median (https://www.tiktok.com/@nicolasacevedo.3c/video/7655506434989264159)
