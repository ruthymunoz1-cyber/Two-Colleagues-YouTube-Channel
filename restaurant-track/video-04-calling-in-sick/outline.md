# Video 4 — Outline (🆕 added in handoff v2)

**Working title:** Calling In Sick — What to Say to Your Manager (Without Feeling Guilty)
**Status:** OUTLINE — full script to be drafted (launch batch, handoff v2)
**Characters:** Maria (server) · James (manager — plays HIMSELF taking the call, a unique angle: viewers hear what the manager actually thinks)

## Concept

A phone-call episode (natural format variation for the launch batch — lower AI-generation risk too: two single-character shots, no object hand-offs). Maria wakes up sick and rehearses the dreaded call. Twist: we see BOTH sides — James's reaction shows managers respect a clear, early, professional call far more than a vague or late one. Directly addresses the guilt/fear immigrant workers feel about calling in sick.

## Phrase Bank (draft — whiteboard recap only)

1. "Hi James, it's Maria. I'm calling because I'm sick today."
2. "I have a fever / a stomach bug / a bad cold." *(name it simply — no oversharing)*
3. "I won't be able to come in for my shift."
4. "I wanted to let you know as early as possible."
5. "I can check if someone can cover my shift."
6. "I'll keep you updated about tomorrow."
7. "Do you need me to bring a doctor's note?"
8. "Thank you for understanding."

## Teaching beats

1. **Skill 1 — Call early, call direct:** texting vs. calling norms; why early beats perfect English
2. **Skill 2 — The 4-sentence formula:** greeting + reason + impact + follow-up (viewers can literally read it off their phone)
3. **Skill 3 — What NOT to do:** over-apologizing, over-explaining symptoms, promising to come in sick
4. **Emotional core:** "You are not a bad worker for being sick" — James says it as the manager; carries the "without feeling guilty" promise of the title
