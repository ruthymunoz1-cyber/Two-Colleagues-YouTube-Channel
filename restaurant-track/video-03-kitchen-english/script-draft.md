## Phrase Bank (the 8 kitchen terms + responses this episode teaches — whiteboard shows them ONLY in the recap)

1. **"86"** = we're out of it → *"Got it — 86 salmon."*
2. **"Behind!" / "Corner!"** = safety callouts → repeat it back as you move
3. **"On the fly"** = urgent, make it fast
4. **"Fire table 12"** = start cooking that table's food NOW
5. **"Running food"** = carrying finished plates to tables
6. **"All day"** = the total count of one dish across all tickets
7. **"Heard!"** = the one-word confirmation the kitchen expects
8. **"How long on table 8?"** = the server's polite status check

**[SCENE: The kitchen pass at full rush. Steam, ticket printer chattering. VOICE OF CHEF = James, machine-gun fast.]**

**CHEF (rapid-fire):** "86-salmon-fire-12-two-burgers-all-day-Maria-run-this-on-the-fly-BEHIND!"

**[FREEZE FRAME on Maria holding a plate, wide-eyed.]**

**MARIA (VO):** I studied English for six years. Nobody — NOBODY — taught me this language.

**MARIA (VO):** The kitchen has its own English. Eight words, and tonight you'll understand every single one. Back to the start of my shift.

## PART 1 — THE FRAME (0:25–1:10)

**[SCENE: Pre-shift. Quiet kitchen. James leans on the pass with coffee; Maria has her notepad out.]**

**JAMES:** Chef told me you froze at the pass last night.

**MARIA:** James, he wasn't speaking English. "Eighty-six"? "All day"? Is it math? Is it time? I just… carried a plate and hoped.

**JAMES (laughs):** Fair. Kitchen English is a code — built for speed, not politeness. Good news: the whole code is about eight words. And there's one golden rule that matters more than all of them.

**MARIA:** Which is?

**JAMES:** The kitchen doesn't need you to be fluent. It needs you to CONFIRM. Every callout gets an answer. Silence is the only wrong response. Ready? I'll be Chef.

---

## PART 2 — SKILL 1: THE CONFIRMATION HABIT (1:10–2:30)

**JAMES (as himself):** One magic word answers almost everything: *"Heard!"* It means: I got your message. Try it.

*(KEY TERM 7 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as chef, fast):** Maria, table 6 is up in two minutes!

**MARIA:** Heard!

**JAMES (as chef):** Dessert for 9 is dying in the window!

**MARIA:** Heard! Going!

**JAMES (as himself):** THAT'S the sound of a server the kitchen trusts. Say nothing, and Chef assumes the message died — so he yells it again, louder, angrier. "Heard" is how you buy peace.

**JAMES (as himself):** Same rule for the safety words. When someone shouts *"Behind!"* they're telling you they're passing behind you — often with something hot or sharp. You say it back — "behind!" — and you do NOT step backwards. Same with *"Corner!"* at blind corners.

*(KEY TERM 2 — spoken repetition; quick physical demo at the corner)*

**JAMES (as chef, passing with a pan):** Behind!

**MARIA (not moving, echoing):** Behind!

**JAMES (as himself):** Perfect. That one's not vocabulary — it's how you keep your skin.

---

## PART 3 — SKILL 2: DECODE THE SHORTHAND (2:30–4:40)

**JAMES (as himself):** Now the code itself. Number one, the famous one: *"86."* It means we are OUT of something. "86 salmon" — no more salmon tonight. Stop selling it, warn your tables.

*(KEY TERM 1 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as chef):** 86 salmon!

**MARIA:** Got it — 86 salmon.

**JAMES (as himself):** And notice you repeated it — confirmation habit, always. Next: *"fire."* Nothing is burning. "Fire table 12" means START COOKING table 12's food now. You'll say it too — when your table finishes their appetizers, you tell the kitchen: "fire mains on 12."

*(KEY TERM 4 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Fire mains on 12.

**JAMES (as himself):** Next: *"on the fly."* Urgent. Skip the line, make it now — usually because something went wrong. "Burger on the fly" means a mistake is being fixed at top speed.

*(KEY TERM 3 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** I need a burger on the fly — I dropped one.

**JAMES (as himself, grinning):** Honest AND correct. Next: *"running food"* — carrying finished plates out. If Chef says "Maria, run this," the plate in the window goes to its table immediately — hot food dies fast.

*(KEY TERM 5 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as himself):** Last decode, the strangest one: *"all day."* It's a total. If one ticket has two burgers and another has three, Chef says "five burgers all day." It's how the kitchen adds across all tickets.

*(KEY TERM 6 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as chef):** How many burgers all day?

**MARIA (checking her pad):** Five burgers all day!

**JAMES (as himself):** You're speaking kitchen.

---

## PART 4 — SKILL 3: ASK WITHOUT ANNOYING THE KITCHEN (4:40–5:50)

**JAMES (as himself):** One more skill — the question servers get wrong. Your table asks "where's our food?" You need an answer from a very busy, very hot human. The phrase: *"How long on table 8?"*

*(KEY TERM 8 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** How long on table 8?

**JAMES (as chef, not looking up):** Three minutes!

**JAMES (as himself):** Short question, number answer, done. What you DON'T do: "Um, excuse me, sorry, the people at table 8 are asking if maybe—" — Chef stopped listening at "um." And never ask twice in three minutes. Ask, get the number, tell your table with confidence: "Three minutes — it's coming out hot."

**MARIA:** So the kitchen isn't rude. It's just… compressed.

**JAMES:** Compressed! Exactly. Every word is doing three words of work. Once you hear it that way, it stops being scary.
