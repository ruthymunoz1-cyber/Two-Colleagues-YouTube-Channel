# Video 6 — Script Draft

**Working title:** "That's Not What I Ordered" — When You're Blamed for the Kitchen's Mistake
**Status:** DRAFT v1 — awaiting script-editor pass
**Level:** B1–B2 — first post-launch episode; Maria's fluency is noticeably higher than in the launch batch
**Characters:** Maria (server) · James (manager, mentor — role-plays the blaming customer AND the chef at the pass)

## Phrase Bank (the 8 phrases this episode teaches — whiteboard shows them ONLY in the recap)

1. "Thank you for letting me know."
2. "We got that wrong — let me fix it for you."
3. "Chef, wrong dish on 12 — I need a remake on the fly." *(callback to Video 3)*
4. "What can I promise them?"
5. "It'll be about five minutes — I'll stay on it."
6. "I'll bring it out myself."
7. "When you get a minute, can we talk about table 12?"
8. "I'm not blaming anyone — I just want to fix it for next time."

## COLD OPEN — HOOK (0:00–0:30)

**[SCENE: Dinner rush. Maria sets a plate down at table 12. VOICE OF CUSTOMER = James — cold and pointed, NOT shouting. A different animal from Video 2's yeller.]**

**CUSTOMER (cold, precise):** Thirty-five minutes. And this — *(taps the plate)* — is not the bolognese. I said it twice. What do you even do with the order after you write it down?

**[FREEZE FRAME on Maria, hand still on the plate.]**

**MARIA (VO):** Angry, I can handle — I've been trained for angry. This is worse. He's blaming ME. For a mistake I didn't make.

**MARIA (VO):** And the truth — "it wasn't me" — is the worst thing I could say. Tonight, two conversations: this table, and the kitchen afterward. Let's learn both.

---

## PART 1 — THE FRAME (0:30–1:25)

**[SCENE: Later that night. Side station, half-rolled silverware. James restocking napkins next to Maria.]**

**JAMES:** I heard table 12 from across the room. You okay?

**MARIA:** I wrote "bolognese" on that ticket, James. I checked it twice. And he looked at me like I cooked the wrong dish on purpose.

**JAMES:** Of course he did. You're the only face he ever sees. Whatever goes wrong in this building lands on whoever's standing at the table.

**MARIA:** That's not fair. Why do I have to wear a mistake that isn't mine?

**JAMES:** You don't wear it. You carry it. There's a difference. Wearing it means it's about you — your fault, your shame. Carrying it means you take the problem to the kitchen and come back with the fix. The mistake was never yours, Maria. The fix is.

**MARIA (beat):** Okay. So what do I say?

**JAMES:** Three skills. At the table — take the fix, not the fault. At the pass — facts in, number out. After the rush — no war. I'll play everyone, as usual.

**MARIA:** You do love the voices.

---

## PART 2 — SKILL 1: TAKE THE FIX, NOT THE FAULT (1:25–3:00)

**[SCENE: Dining room, after close. James at a table as the blaming customer. Maria approaches.]**

**JAMES (as himself, aside):** First, the trap. I'll blame you to your face — you answer with the truth.

**JAMES (as customer, cold):** This is not what I ordered. How hard is it to write down one dish?

**MARIA:** It wasn't me — the kitchen made a mistake.

**JAMES (as himself):** True. And terrible.

**MARIA:** But it IS true!

**JAMES:** He sees ONE restaurant, Maria — wearing your apron. "It wasn't me" sounds like "not my problem." And blaming the kitchen makes us a restaurant that fights with itself. Nobody orders dessert at that restaurant.

**MARIA:** So I say it was my fault? I lie?

**JAMES:** Never. Don't take the fault for food you didn't cook — say it every shift and one day you'll believe it. The third answer starts with four strange words: *"Thank you for letting me know."*

*(KEY PHRASE 1 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Thank you for letting me know.

**JAMES:** Feel that? No defense, no confession. Now the pronoun that saves your night — not "I," not "they": *"We got that wrong — let me fix it for you."*

*(KEY PHRASE 2 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** We got that wrong — let me fix it for you.

**JAMES:** "We" is the restaurant — and the restaurant DID get it wrong. Honest. Now put your angry-customer training in the middle and you've got the full table sequence. You'll run it live before the night's over.

---

## PART 3 — SKILL 2: THE MESSENGER RUN — FACTS IN, NUMBER OUT (3:00–4:50)

**[SCENE: The kitchen pass. James steps behind it as the chef.]**

**JAMES (as himself):** Now the walk — carrying a plate back for a mistake that isn't yours. Most servers spend that walk building their case. By the pass, they're a lawyer. Wrong job. Show me the lawyer version.

**MARIA:** Chef, YOU got table 12 wrong. I wrote bolognese, clear as day, so this one is on you—

**JAMES (as chef, bristling):** I cook what I see! You want to check the board? Now? During service?

**JAMES (as himself):** Now the food is late AND the kitchen is angry. During a rush, chef can do one thing with your words: cook. So give him only words that cook — dish, table, speed: *"Chef, wrong dish on 12 — I need a remake on the fly."*

*(KEY PHRASE 3 — spoken repetition; "on the fly" is the Video 3 callback)*

**MARIA:** Chef, wrong dish on 12 — I need a bolognese on the fly.

**JAMES (as chef, already moving):** Heard. Bolognese on the fly.

**JAMES (as himself):** Kitchen English — you already speak it. And notice what was missing: whose fault it was. That question can wait. Before you leave the pass, one more: *"What can I promise them?"*

*(KEY PHRASE 4 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** What can I promise them?

**JAMES (as chef):** Five minutes.

**JAMES (as himself):** Now you own a number. Back at the table you're not apologizing anymore — you're reporting: *"It'll be about five minutes — I'll stay on it."*

*(KEY PHRASE 5 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** It'll be about five minutes — I'll stay on it.

**JAMES:** And when it's ready: *"I'll bring it out myself."* He watched the mistake arrive in your hands — let him watch the fix arrive the same way.

*(KEY PHRASE 6 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** I'll bring it out myself.

---

## PART 4 — SKILL 3: AFTER THE RUSH — WITHOUT STARTING A WAR (4:50–5:55)

**[SCENE: Kitchen, end of night. Quiet. James as the chef, wiping down the pass.]**

**JAMES (as himself, aside):** Last skill — the talk almost nobody has, which is why the same mistake happens every Friday. Wait for the rush to die. Never in front of guests, never opening with "you": *"When you get a minute, can we talk about table 12?"*

*(KEY PHRASE 7 — spoken repetition)*

**MARIA:** Hey chef — when you get a minute, can we talk about table 12?

**JAMES (as chef, guard up):** …The board was full. Tickets were doubled up.

**MARIA:** I'm not blaming anyone — I just want to fix it for next time.

*(KEY PHRASE 8 — spoken repetition)*

**JAMES (as chef, beat — the guard drops):** …Two tickets stuck together. I plated off the wrong one. That one's mine, Maria.

**JAMES (as chef):** When the board's full, call your pasta out loud and I'll echo it back. Deal?

**MARIA:** Deal.

**JAMES (as himself):** Notice — she didn't get an apology. She got a SYSTEM. "I'm not blaming anyone" lets someone say "that one's mine" without losing face. Corner people and they defend till midnight. Give them room, and they hand you the truth.

---

## PART 5 — THE PAYOFF (5:55–6:55)

**[SCENE: Return to the cold-open moment. Table 12. Same cold voice.]**

**MARIA (VO):** Table 12. Same night, second chance.

**JAMES (as customer, cold — same line as cold open):** Thirty-five minutes. And this is not the bolognese. What do you even do with the order after you write it down?

**MARIA (steady):** Thank you for letting me know. You're right — that's not what you ordered. We got that wrong, and I'm fixing it right now.

**[THE WALK — table to pass, one continuous shot. No music, no dialogue. Her face: not shame — purpose.]**

**MARIA (at the pass):** Chef, wrong dish on 12 — I need a bolognese on the fly.

**JAMES (as chef, off-screen):** Heard. Bolognese on the fly.

**MARIA:** What can I promise them?

**JAMES (as chef):** Five minutes.

**[Back at the table.]**

**MARIA:** It'll be about five minutes, and I'll bring it out myself.

**JAMES (as customer, cooling):** …Fine. Thank you.

**[DISSOLVE: Maria sets down the bolognese.]**

**MARIA:** One bolognese. Thank you for your patience.

**JAMES (as customer, beat):** The wait was bad. But you handled it well.

**MARIA (to camera, quiet):** He never found out whose mistake it was. He didn't need to — he needed his dinner, and one person in the middle, carrying the fix.

## Production Notes

- **Emotional beat to protect:** the Part 1 exchange — Maria: "Why do I have to wear a mistake that isn't mine?" / James: "You don't wear it. You carry it. […] The mistake was never yours, Maria. The fix is." No music under it; hold on Maria a beat after. This is the line viewers will send to a coworker.
- **THE WALK is the signature shot:** the story card's viral evidence is literally "that walk when it wasn't your mistake" (426K views, 57.9x median). In the payoff, the table-to-pass walk is one continuous take, silent, her expression turning from stung to purposeful. It's also the natural Shorts clip.
- **James's registers:** the customer here is COLD and pointed — quiet contempt, not volume. It must sound clearly different from Video 2's yeller; that contrast is the episode's positioning. The chef register carries over from Video 3: clipped, urgent, zero malice — busy, not mean.
- **Continuity:** table 12 is deliberately the same trouble table as V2/V3/V5 — the channel's unlucky table; keep the number. Deliberate phrase callbacks, kept to callbacks (not re-taught): "You're right — that's not what you ordered" and "Thank you for your patience" from V2; "on the fly" and "Heard" from V3. James's "put your angry-customer training in the middle" flags the V2 reuse in-world.
- **Post-launch note:** first regular-cadence episode after the launch batch. Maria's baseline fluency is visibly higher — direct her reads steadier and quicker than V1–V2; her problem in this episode is never comprehension, it's not having the script for an unfair moment.
- **Whiteboard:** recap only. **Captions:** `.srt` upload only, never burned in.
- **Cross-language port:** "Dos Colegas Español: Cuando te culpan por el error de la cocina" — the blame-transfer moment scored across EN and ES sources in the sweep; scenario logic ports unchanged.
