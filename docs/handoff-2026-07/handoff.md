## ⚠️ FLAGGED RISK — READ FIRST

The production approach below (realistic cinematic video) was requested after a prior attempt at full AI-generated character video produced **detached body parts and missing furniture/environment**. That failure mode is a known current limitation of multi-character AI video generation (Kling, Veo, Sora, and vidIQ's own video models) — not a one-off glitch. Realistic + cinematic + two characters interacting with physical objects (menus, plates, trays, a counter) is the **highest-risk combination** for that exact failure to recur.

**Recommendation before full production:** generate one 15–30 second test clip of the two characters interacting with a single object (e.g., handing over a menu) before committing a full episode to this approach. If it fails, fall back to the **still-image + voiceover + captions** format (character cards, gentle pan/zoom, real B-roll for environment) — which vidIQ itself flagged as the more reliable standard for faceless channels.

## 2. Channel: Two Colleagues English

- **Format:** Dialogue-based workplace English between two colleagues
- **Target audience:** Immigrant workers, intermediate English (B1–B2), can read English but lack speaking confidence in professional settings
- **Channel promise:** "I help working professionals speak English confidently at work by showing realistic office/workplace conversations between two colleagues — not textbook drills."
- **Niche decision (vidIQ, July 2026):** Workplace English beat General English (saturated) and Spanish Fluency (fragmented, needs rebrand). Channel name "Two Colleagues" already telegraphs the value prop — no rebrand needed.

| 1 | Restaurant / Hospitality | 171x | Universal immigrant entry point; existing content is customer-side only ("how to order food"); worker-side content (taking orders, angry customers, kitchen English, shift handovers) is a content gap. One script ports across ~10 languages. |

## 5. Production Approach — LOCKED per Ruthy's direction (July 2026)

- **Format:** realistic, cinematic AI-generated video (not still-image cards)
- **Voiceover:** two distinct voices, one per character, so dialogue sounds like two people actually talking
- **Animated whiteboard element:** animated on-screen words/vocabulary appear during the dialogue, whiteboard-style
- **End-segment recap (required in every episode):** a listening-comprehension / vocabulary recap using the animated whiteboard, with one or two characters taking turns — one asks a question, pauses, then gives the answer, reinforcing the episode's key vocabulary/phrases

> See risk flag at top of document — this approach has a higher failure risk than the still-image format based on the prior test result, and should be piloted on a short clip first.

## 6. What vidIQ Can / Cannot Do (per vidIQ's own capability statement, July 2026)

**Can do:**
- Full scripts (dialogue between two characters, with scene directions, timestamps, hooks, CTAs)
- Dual AI voiceovers, one per character
- Character reference images (consistent illustrated characters)
- Full video composition (voiceovers + captions + character images + scene transitions + B-roll)
- Branded animated captions

**Cannot do:**
- Full AI talking-head avatars with real-time lip sync (HeyGen/Synthesia-style). Still images can be generated and animated across scenes, but not real-time lip-synced talking video.

This is a factor in why the still-image format is the more reliable fallback if the cinematic approach doesn't hold up in testing.
