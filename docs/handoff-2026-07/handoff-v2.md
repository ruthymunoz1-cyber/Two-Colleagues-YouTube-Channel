## ⚠️ FLAGGED RISK — READ FIRST

*(unchanged from v1)* The realistic-cinematic approach follows a prior attempt that produced detached body parts and missing furniture/environment — a known limitation of multi-character AI video generation (Kling, Veo, Sora, vidIQ's models), not a one-off glitch. Realistic + cinematic + two characters + physical objects is the highest-risk combination.

**Before full production:** generate one 15–30 second test clip of the duo interacting with a single object. If it fails, fall back to still-image + voiceover + captions (character cards, gentle pan/zoom, real B-roll) — vidIQ's flagged reliable standard for faceless channels.

## 5. Production Approach — LOCKED per Ruthy's direction (July 2026)

- **Format:** realistic, cinematic AI-generated video (not still-image cards)
- **Voiceover:** two distinct voices, one per character
- **🆕 v2 — Animated whiteboard element:** used **only in the end-segment recap** — NOT present during the main dialogue portion of the episode
- **🆕 v2 — Captions:** **no captions/subtitles baked into the video.** Subtitles are optional for the viewer (on/off via YouTube's native caption toggle), never hardcoded into the visuals
- **End-segment recap (required in every episode):** listening-comprehension/vocabulary recap using the animated whiteboard; one asks a question, pauses, then gives the answer. **This is the only portion of the video where the whiteboard appears.**
- **🆕 v2 — Tooling caveat:** vidIQ is the current **default** tool for this pipeline, **not a locked-in requirement**. If Fable identifies a better-suited tool for any part (script, voiceover, character video, or the whiteboard recap specifically), that substitution is open — **the approach and constraints are what's locked, not the vendor.**

> Risk flag still applies — pilot on a short clip first.
